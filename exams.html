Complete the original exercise, then answer its follow-up aloud. Take up to one minute before checking the review points.

Investigate missing employee numbers

How many 2016 rows have a missing Employee_Number, and what total Gross_Wages do those rows contain? What can you conclude?

Examiner follow-up A colleague suggests excluding all rows with a missing Employee_Number before reporting total gross wages. Would you do that? What would you check first?

Review points
  • Keep the recorded wages in the total unless there is evidence that the transactions should be corrected or excluded.
  • Flag the affected rows separately and show how much the total would fall if they were excluded.
  • Reconcile the flagged wages to the payroll register and check another identifier against the employee master before drawing a conclusion.

Find directors of films rated 8.0 or higher

Return the names of people who directed at least one film rated 8.0 or higher. Include each person ID once. Row order is optional.

Examiner follow-up Suppose a director has two qualifying films. Two different directors also share the same name. Would SELECT DISTINCT name solve both problems? How would you verify your result?

Review points
  • The join can produce multiple rows for one director when more than one film qualifies.
  • DISTINCT on the name merges different people with the same name. Deduplicate by person ID, then display the names.
  • Inspect person IDs in an intermediate result and check that each qualifying ID appears exactly once.

Compare sales on game and non-game days

Use the game-day model to find the predicted dollar difference between a game day and a non-game day. Give the difference to two decimals and explain what it measures.

Examiner follow-up The manager says game days caused the higher predicted sales. What else could explain the difference, and what comparison would you run to check it?

Review points
  • The coefficient compares average sales on game and non-game days in this dataset.
  • If games cluster on busy days of the week, the weekday mix could explain part of the difference.
  • Compare game and non-game days on the same weekday, or add weekday controls. The adjusted comparison still describes an association.
